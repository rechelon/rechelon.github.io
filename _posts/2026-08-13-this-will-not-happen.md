## Part 1: The Blue November

It's amazing how few people realized what was going to happen.

There was a sense, of course, that the old US empire was on the verge of *something* catastrophic, but even as all the telltale signs were lit in neon, basically everyone refused to engage in detailed speculation. Many commented that civil war, internal waves of genocide, infrastructural collapse, and mass death seemed on the way. But such concepts denote endings, not beginnings or transformations. Aside from the fascists chaotically moving to seize the 2026 election, various political camps girded themselves defensively, but almost no one prepared in a proactive sense.

And no one, absolutely no one—including themselves—expected the Leninists to win.

You are no doubt familiar with the triumphalist Leninist historiography that dominated US propaganda over the following century, and perhaps counter-narratives that dismiss the old US empire as a weak regime too soft on vast hordes of Leninist workers. But the truth is right up to the revolution the Leninists were of vanishingly small number and relevance.

After their financiers withdrew with the end of the Cold War, the remaining Leninists in the US languished for decades as a marginal fringe of even the radical left. Most leftists laughed at them for perpetually trying to apply the same outdated model from a century prior. Yet Lenin himself had been something of a stubborn historical cosplayer, obsessing over the French Revolution, unimaginatively trying to reproduce its structures and operating within the conceptual vernacular it spawned. And—while it certainly wasn't the result of some inevitable historical materialist march—it just so happened that conditions in the 250-year-old US empire were surprisingly close to those classic revolutions. By the fall of 2026, mass unemployment and ratcheting food scarcity were really getting going. This was partially a result of the 2020s AI race and the mad fascist president’s tariffs, but, just like had happened in Lenin’s time with the Russian empire, the US empire had launched a war against a country it assumed was obviously inferior and lost to devastating consequences.

Similarly, while public support for the ruling regime had been strong, and there were still massive fascist forces mobilizing in defense of it, by November 2026 its public image was in a tailspin. The recent seizure of Cuba had gone a lot better than perpetually dragging war in Iran, and the regime struggled to trumpet this new success as a sign of strength. But the “victory” came off as desperate, venal, and cringe. The delegation that arrived with US Marines to oversee the remaining government in divvying up land and writing new laws for the starving island were no Chicago School boys but tacky and unprepared grifters. Soon even conservatives were joining jokes about the new Cuban regime, indeed the term we still use for it, “Meyerfest,” derived from a conspiracy popular among fascists about one of the Trump delegates and in reference to an earlier Caribbean boondoggle called "Fyrefest." If the 90s privatization in the USSR that killed millions was tragedy, the privatization of Cuba was total farce.

Further, as coverage of expats in Miami leaving to pillage the motherland circulated, a consequence emerged that the regime was not prepared for: a mass push in the ranks of their fascist base to celebrate this as their "remigration" model of ethnic cleansing. "If we can do this to the browns in Florida, why can't we do this to every other brown?" Fascist media personalities latched on and started filming from Miami, stirring up conflict with cuban-americans for views. The vice president steadfastly tried to hold together online right-wing factions, but this came at the cost of a post sending shockwaves through the Cuban community and led to catastrophic polling results. Aggressive AI intervention into what was shown in most social media feeds proved too slow to stop the backlash.

There were so many surprises and blowups around the 2026 elections that historians continue to research and debate their relative impacts, but unquestionably the biggest was President Trump threatening to nuke Japan after they backed out of a trade deal. Now there was no way the dying ruler could have imagined *that* among all his rabid provocations would actually bring consequences, and of course he tried to spin it as the overly sensitive liberals getting offended for no reason to a bit of macho ribbing, but the international consequences kept compounding in ways that stole the headlines. And the takeaway to a hundred million apolitical Americans was simple: as bad as the economy is right now, it's gonna get worse, because of the mad king.

Yet, as we all know, when the dust settled on November 4th, the intervention of the Supreme Court and the "Independent Polling Irregularities Commission" mobilizations meant that the centrist liberal party—the Democrats—were left with FEWER seats in the lower chamber of the empire’s legislature. And yes, infamously, two people died trying to cast their votes, although the dead white girl overshadowed the other.

I think it’s important to correct a common misunderstanding about the 2026 elections, because now it’s broadly assumed these were elections for the presidency, but they were actually a more *minor* election in the old system always scheduled *between* the presidential elections called “midterms” that only determined the apportionment of minor representatives in their legislative body. It's amusing looking over these old records because of how breathlessly they refer to these "midterms" when, as we know there was very little further "term" ahead, nor would there be any other national elections in the US, ever again.

But the most important result in the 2026 elections wasn't the further step in Trump's power consolidation before he planned to suspend the constitution in 2028\. It was the victory of "the DSA candidates." The nomenclature is notoriously inaccurate, as many of said candidates were not members of the "Democratic Socialists of America," just slightly more progressive than the norm, but the nomenclature became widespread and we continue to use it to this day.

The success of these candidates might have been eclipsed by the wider theft of the election, but the liberal outrage machine had been ramping up for months in advance and editorials had already been written, so as the shootings and the Supreme Court rulings held the top media attention, just below them was the entire establishment losing their minds over the scourge of "socialists," who simultaneously were going viral in everyone's feeds with charisma and authentic smiles. The liberal media found themselves incapable of influencing public narratives against both right and left, and so only ended up highlighting both and marginalizing any possible liberal figures of gravitas, elevating "the DSA candidates"—including previously elected politicians like Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez—into icons of progressive victory amid defeat. This virtually shut out liberal pundits struggling to regain narrative control in the following weeks.

A handful of liberal orgs had spent months preparing to lead people in mass protests should the election be stolen, but they had no plan for anything beyond that. Ideologically there was a widespread myth in the late American empire—one that had been widely cultivated by the establishment after MLK Jr.'s assassination to suppress insurgent social movements—wherein getting large numbers of people to walk in a political parade was somehow in-and-of-itself a success and a way to enact change. Since liberals had drunk their own koolaid, they simply didn't have any further strategic analysis. “Protests” were treated as something like elections where victory was magically achieved by turnout, not tools of mass mobilization or threats to leverage in further steps. And organizationally, every incentive within the liberal orgs was to build their own organizational standing, accumulating contact lists, donors, and volunteers, not actually bring down the Trump regime. 

By every right this is how things should have ended, some mass rallies for a week or two and then a return to chattering on social media where liberals prepared to vote "even harder" in a supposedly upcoming "next election." Another two years of backbiting and performative howling as this or that thing was demolished.

But, as we know, the "organizers" of these protests very quickly lost control of them, and instead of liberals *going home* after the marches, they surged into local government buildings—even some federal ones—and started occupying them.

These occupations are now often presented by both liberal and communist historians as inevitable and natural developments, but it's worth noting that not just the liberal organizers but the Leninist cadres with some control or entryist presence were all *opposed* to this turn to occupation, and indeed in many situations fought against it. It was almost always *anarchists* who took the initiative and showed enraged liberal protesters that there was more possible than marching in circles.

The result was fiercely popular occupations in a ton of cities with sometimes literally tens of thousands of people inside and around government buildings of various relevance. The liberal organizers rapidly worked to regain control and standing in these occupations, printing and distributing mass placards that narrowed down on two isolated demands around electoral recounts. But this barely contained the simmering anger in the occupation camps, where folks were increasingly talking about wild things like new constitutional conventions. At this point, if they were going to get out ahead and fulfill their role as counter-insurgents containing the streets, the liberal Democrats really needed to take a much more aggressive posture.

Yet there was almost no institutional or cultural memory much less appetite within the liberal Democrats for escalation, and instead of joining the protests and contesting the election, a large swathe of the Democratic establishment desperately bolted to talk down, discredit, and sign whatever they could licensing force to stop the protests. 

At the most pivotal week of street mobilizations they focused on public collaborations with the GOP in getting one of the DSA candidates banned from office over something they had said.

Meanwhile Trump screamed nonstop promises to murder all the establishment Democrats, and the more establishment rightwing media ecosystem focused everything on equating the Democratic establishment with the protesters to justify liquidating the leading Dems. Right after he succeeded in overseeing the expulsion of one of the DSA candidates, the liberal “House Minority Leader” was himself arrested for Treason.

Consistently, the more Trump promised to murder congressional Democrats, the more desperately they struggled to differentiate themselves from their base and help suppress the protests. 

It's important to understand that this was highly rational in a sense, given their evaluation of material conditions. One Elissa Slotkin of Michigan—who would somehow survive the following years and escape to exile in Sweden—told interviewers towards the end of her life that there was a widespread sense among Democrats in congress that street pressure outside the state had zero chance of winning because "we're not hicks with guns" and thus the only conceivable means to personal survival was to fall over themselves proving their loyalty to Trump, hoping to wheedle out some concessions or friction on the margins while being allowed to stay in office and out of prison. And that evaluation seems somewhat reasonable, in the sense that Slotkin was not one of the "Congressional 21" ziptied and frogmarched out of Capitol Hill in the coming weeks.

Certainly many of the occupations were quickly broken up and forced—or persuaded by local politicians—to retreat from government buildings. And the legacy media remained largely lockstep with the establishment democrats, openly panicking that any substantive resistance meant assured destruction, that it would only "legitimize" the escalation of the liquidations by fascists. Of course, we can see that this conceptualization of "legitimacy" was completely disconnected from the sort of material consequences that the streets represented. To worry about Trump's consolidation of power being seen as legitimate implies that there would have been some victory if it was seen as illegitimate, but the potential violent force being mobilized by the streets were the only mechanism that could have made such "illegitimacy" have any consequences.

Again, it's important to note that the liberals *could* have consolidated their own power during this period, but repeatedly chose not to, both through a lack of imagination and in fear of a Left that did not really have its footing yet. At one point, the stenographers of what had been a long-standing regime outlet, "The New York Times," printed a headline warning about "imminent communist insurgency"—which you're no doubt familiar with, since it has been reproduced in memes for generations as a Cassandra-like prediction—but there was almost no appreciable "communist" presence in the protests when the headline was written.

And to be clear, while the streets presented *potential* force, at this point there was far more force being brought to bear by the regime. Even before the election, Trump had been deploying military units, leveraging an old edict called "the Insurrection Act" and getting accidentally bogged down into legal battles around a poorly thought out attempt to formally take control over local police. Local and federal police collaborated in brutalizing and occasionally gunning down the protesters, in one case setting fire to a government building while barricading the protesters inside while they burned to death, but this didn't break the back of the occupations. And there was a very predictable spiral whereby the regime would repeatedly decide that it had been too weak, squeamish and restrained in slaughter before, and only needed to increase the death toll to break the protests… only to be surprised when the protests were strengthened by their latest escalation.

Despite the failures in the courts of their initial strategy to consolidate direct control, escalating conflict saw local police stop engaging in any pretense of following the local liberal politicians supposedly above them in the command hierarchy of the US empire. Two mayors were arrested by their own police (without charges besides "treason") to wide applause. There was a small spate of relatively negative coverage when the child of an insufficiently compliant city councilor was abducted by local police as a hostage, but, like countless other examples of the cops threatening their politician "superiors" before, the story never went very wide. 

Throughout his first and second reign, Trump had been deeply constrained by his own media consumption habits and the incentives of those media producers, so he consistently failed to recognize opportunities to assert direct control, even when local police were basically promising to follow his orders and dissolve the last pretenses of the old constitutional order. Only at the end of November did it finally start to leak into Trump's media diet that there were cops standing up to their "traitorous" local orders and he finally realized he could bypass the courts and bureaucracy via social media posts commanding the police. This is when the remit of the infamous Joint Terrorism Taskforces started dramatically expanding and presidential staffers began flying out to set up camp in local police departments. Forensic historians believe this is when the very first secret mass graves started, typically supplied via packed buses that ran directly from snatch squads at the protests, bypassing documentation.

In parallel, rightwing media were engaged in increasingly frantic discussion of how there was clearly a conspiracy behind the failure of the police to have already executed every protester. So while the JTTF kill squads were still working in secrecy, they started publicly assembling crews of "Republicans With Democrats for Security" to do the police's job for them. These were pitched as ostensibly "bipartisan" citizen volunteer committees in defense of law and order, but the RWDS initials were intended to be obvious to many contemporaries, while still allowing credulous conservatives to join up, sincerely believing it to be an unhinged leftwing conspiracy that "RWDS" ever stood for "Right Wing Death Squads." This was a thin veneer, but it’s one they nonetheless worked at: creating a barrage of media content from people pretending to be liberals or democrats who thanked them profusely for helping them retake and restore the Democratic Party from the communists/cartels who had seized it.

Largely ignoring both the JTTFs and RWDSs, the liberal media remained steadfast in condemning protesters who responded to force with force, but there was some back-and-forth dancing, with, for example, a video of police grenades blowing out the windows of a house of bystanders obliging talking heads to recognize and trail public outrage. But, despite their best efforts to tamp down energies in the streets, the ever-present background of protesters being murdered by JTTFs and the RWDSs started getting more attention, and then video of a couple protesters firing back on cops blew up to the point where pollsters revealed that 57% of the nation supported the protesters firing back.

You will not be surprised to hear that, in a retread of what we talked about in our episode on the earlier 2020 uprising, well-funded nonprofits quickly paid small-time actors and influencers to pretend to be protest leaders, and their photogenic pandering was initially very effective at corralling first-time-protesters. They used this influence to coordinate the formation of a nationwide brand of peace police, doing trainings in dozens of cities and handing out impressively expensive kit. Well-meaning liberals were rapidly drawn into their influence network because they seemed serious, provided good resources, firm organization, and a way to plug in that didn’t feel as off-putting as the existing leftist orgs, defined by esoteric language and backbiting.

These peace police and the "Blue Wave Electoral Integrity Guards" (or “Blue Guard”) legions they formed into over November were a hail mary to suppress the protests, funded by a few billionaires in collaboration with liberal centrists, but they rapidly came to play a much bigger role in the coming revolutionary struggles, if only because of all the kit that they bought and managed, from food to radios to trauma kits. 

Although at this point, in late November, the Blue Guards were not yet armed with guns. Just as Democrat governors studiously refused to call state units even as police and paramilitary forces increasingly moved to depose them because as one governor scoffingly put it, a day before he was executed with his family, "violence is the tool of the other side."

So, by the end of November, armed pickup convoys of RWDSs from the suburbs were regularly raiding into cities to shoot up the Blue Guard controlled occupations, with the very explicit collaboration of local cops, and the Blue Guards refused to allow people to get armed in response, instead escalating internal purges to try and find "Proud Boy infiltrators" which inevitably meant any leftist militants. By this point almost none of the activists who helped lead the initial seizure of government buildings were still around in the camps.

The nonprofit funding of the Blue Guards and their presence in the protests were of course noticed, not just by leftists, but also by the right, who happily promoted conspiracies that the protesters were "a plot by billionaire communists to seize the government." But this right-wing narrative only reinforced the Blue Guards legitimacy among the protesters and marginalized leftists with the same criticisms of their funding.

This hostile exclusion of anarchists and explicit leftists had the cumulative effect of filtering out everyone with experience besides the assortment of fringe Leninist groupsicles and factions who specialized in secretive entryism and control over liberal mass organizations. These cadres had long prepared for precisely this sort of situation, and the more the liberal-centrist forces pushed to consolidate control over the protests and marginalize leftists, the more they functionally handed power to these Leninists. There are still recordings of early webconferences where right-liberal political operatives and funders excitedly embrace their supposedly loyal protest managers reporting on their efforts to shut out "the radicals," unaware that these managers are themselves a roster of some of the most famous Leninist figures that would come to power and in multiple instances personally sign the execution papers of their "leadership" in those calls.

But of course none of these maneuverings were then visible to the rank and file of the Blue Guards or the much wider base of the protests.

We’re not going to drill too deeply into the names of the Leninist factions at play in the early days of the Blue Guards, because while there were existing Leninist groups with aspirational names like “Party for Socialism and Liberation” or “Freedom Road Socialist Organization Fight Back” whose national group chats and command hierarchies played significant roles in the Blue Guards, many of their individuals operated in a very opportunistic manner in these early heady days, leading to a network of personal alliances and betrayals that muddled existing organizational divisions, while creating new tensions that held back consolidation. 

While the Guards' own ranks supported "order" and liked the sense of legitimacy and hierarchy provided by a single nationwide organization, they were not in any sense ideologically in line with Blue Guard central, or even really aware of how divergent their interests were.

Similarly, the wider protests increasingly viewed themselves as in an existential battle against the Democratic establishment. This meant that the camps consistently provided massive support for the DSA candidates constantly traveling between camps, mostly managing to avoid interception and arrest. Nor did Blue Guards in any sense hold a uniform monopoly on protest security; many camps had multiple volunteer peace police groups operating simultaneously, as different networks and organizations jockeyed. Often a single individual who declared themselves protest "security" wore many organizational affiliations simultaneously, responding to competing loci of decisionmaking.

Further, these self-appointed security forces were also just one of many loci of power in the protests, with bitter and chaotic power struggles present in many other arenas like social media accounts and camp kitchens. On December 2nd, there was honestly no guarantee that the Blue Guards would have remained relevant, or indeed that anything would come of their Leninist middle managers.

## Part 2: The Red December

And so we reach The Day Of Two Armies, December 3rd 2026, one month into the occupations and various conflicts across the urban spaces of the US, when President Trump gave the order to execute every protester in DC.

He had, of course, given variants of this order before, but this is the one that stuck.

As we’ve covered, the police had been murdering protesters since the beginning of November, but their efforts were scattershot and reactive. Trump's new order was to—in the words of his deputy chief of staff—“systematically eliminate these criminal degenerates from the gene pool." To avoid interference, it was broadcast loudly and directly along multiple channels. And, to the surprise of nearly everyone, local DC police balked. Suddenly bureaucratic protocols mattered, and even the rapid removal and imprisonment of Chief Jeffery Carroll didn't impact the flow of overly formal foot-dragging messages.

While some, like Carroll, would try to later spin careers as the first police to stand up to Trump, historians are pretty universal in evaluating this surge of reticence as less a product of moral objection than trying to pass the buck. What was being asked was, in their minds, beyond their job description. Laziness, cowardice, and ass-covering were ubiquitous among the police forces of the old US empire and the audacious amount of work implied by the order was too much too soon for them.

Similarly, the various federal agencies that had been deputized into operating as beat cops harassing homeless encampments in the nation’s capital over the prior year largely evaporated in a cloud of sick days and transparent excuses.

The head of the US armed forces, Pete Hegseth—amid drunken public rants promising retribution on any local cops who disobeyed direct orders—responded by quickly moving several more military formations into DC, lining the streets with military vehicles as a show of force to the boss.

An armored division from the Pennsylvania National Guard allowed themselves to be photographed in formation but were slow in proceeding into the city proper. Tanks that had been shipped in from Fort Riley and gathered at Fort Myer forced their way down Pennsylvania Avenue and were ordered to point their guns at the John A. Wilson Building where the main locus of protesters was gathered.

While the exact command sequence is unknown, at 1:05pm eastern, a single M830A1 round was fired through the second story, blowing out critical masonry structure, tearing through the unoccupied building behind and causing a blast pressure that blew out the lungs of many not directly ripped apart or crushed under the collapsing structure. No further cannon fire came but there were bursts of small gun discharges recorded on the streams over the next two minutes.

The burst of bright red blood staining the side of a collapsing conference room immediately became an iconic historical image, and is typically used to signify the beginning of Red December.

Cellular networks in DC went almost entirely down at around 1:07pm. Almost everyone of any grouping on the ground believed this to be the result of government action, but we now know it was simply an infrastructural collapse from overactivity.

What did get out before the network went down was a short clip from Sergeant Samuel Smith in front of other men in the DC National Guard griping, "fuck this shit; we aint doing this."

It is completely unclear what degree of seriousness this video recording to a friend held, but it was rapidly shared across social media, instantly generating the widespread *impression* that the DC National Guard, which had always been reticent about the occupation, was in revolt.

It's broadly understood by historians that the incipient revolution would almost certainly have crumbled from the DC slaughter had the head of the armed forces simply given the orders to kill protesters and nothing more. After all, he had moved aggressively to purge the military leadership and they had complied. While the officer corps broadly disliked the Trump regime, they were hardly liberal and huge swathes of the rank-and-file subscribed to the populist side of the fascist movement. FOX News played 24/7 in all military facilities and the more educated officer corps were obsessively loyal to an honor code of neutrality that saw diligently obeying orders as a centrist defense of law and order.

And on the other side, Leftists had done pretty much zero outreach to the military. There was almost no consumable media packaged for them, no concerted effort at messaging, and certainly no mapping of the structures and tensions within the military. Not a single revolution in history has succeeded without the help of a revolt within the military, and the Left was in no remote sense sincerely preparing for a revolution. Up until the very last minute, every incentive was on building their own standing in contrast to liberals and other leftist currents, and this aligned all leftist discourse and propaganda towards shows of moral purity. There was nothing to be gained from being caught even remotely sympathetic to the baby-killing attack dogs of US imperialism, any more than there was to be gained doing outreach at nazi rallies. To be fair, in peacetime such sympathies and outreach can erode and undermine long-term radical movements, opening them up to corruption and entryism. And, after centuries of marginalization in the old US empire, the left quite reasonably believed it was engaged in such long-term movement building, not on the edge of an actual revolution. The Leninists were essentially no different from the rest of the left in this.

Similarly, all throughout the lead up to Red December, liberals had staunchly refused to consider or plan for the possibility of a contest of material force. They consistently decided that the maintenance of the state was of such importance that it did not matter what that state looked like, or how fascist it got. They were not going to even think about staging a coup or engaging in anything like revolt. And so, even though disenchanted members of the military and intelligence communities formed informal networks of grousing and shit talk—we’ll talk about the infamous Virginia Group Chat in a minute—these networks went largely without any contact or feelers from the liberal establishment.

All of this adds up in one direction: the military didn't *like* being ordered to massacre the eleven thousand or so protesters in and around the DC building occupations, but there's every indication they would have done so with only slight grumbling and foot-dragging.

But every revolution needs idiocy at the top to work. And so Hegseth’s response was to declare the DC National Guard to be engaged in treason and order a detachment of Marines from Quantico to liquidate them.

It's unclear what he was thinking. In the initial aftermath practically everyone assumed the order came from Trump—after all the entire project of getting "loyal generals, like Hitler's" had gotten its main impetus from Trump's frustrations at not being able to slaughter protesters during the 2020 uprising. But recordings from inside his palace show Trump was surprised. This initiative was all Hegseth.

What records we have of the choices made by the generals are notoriously thin, thanks to the default operational security of the military, deliberate decisions to destroy records later, and the untrustworthiness of later testimony. It's clear that many regiments did not understand what was happening and their information environment was often completely polluted with rumors to the polar opposite of facts on the ground.

What is conclusively known is this: by 5pm the Alabama National Guard had been separated and fired on a small detachment of the DC National Guard. The 273rd Military Police Company and the 3rd US Infantry Regiment had softly refused orders and then, upon hearing that the DC National Guard was being fired on by two helicopters, declared themselves in public noncompliance.

Uncertainty over what was happening and the danger they were in led the 3rd Infantry to then actually fire first on an arriving detachment of Marines.

Scenes of US troops firing on US troops flickered into wider social media and were breathlessly commented on, alongside some rushed AI footage created by conservatives to call for the liquidation of the 3rd Infantry that ended up ironically convincing other military units that widespread military revolt was in place against a rogue Trumpist general and they had to join their brethren to restore honor and stop the unlawful situation.

It's also totally unclear if the Leftist crew that invaded FOX News and executed Laura Ingraham—infamously mid-shout about the need to “kill them all”—at the same time did so with the strategic intent of cutting off the main information source of servicemen, or whether it was purely a reactive effort of unthinking "adventurism," as later Leninist historians would claim. We have evidence that two anarchists involved survived the kill squad that retook the building, but at least one of them would be later caught and liquidated by Leninists in the coming regime. What digital artifacts we have from Anarchists at the time indicate they were largely convinced the FOX action was perpetrated by wingnuts freshly radicalized from online content and without ties to the existing movement.

Either way, the end result was a completely unmoored information environment that ended up facilitating mass military revolt, even if most everyone involved didn't see themselves as in revolt. By 10:38pm Hegseth was dead (whether by his own hand or not, we will probably never know) and power in the Pentagon was rapidly moving in a direction autonomous from Trump’s circle. 

More broadly, 76 servicemen were dead and at least two thousand protesters had been gunned down or blown apart, countless more were injured.

In response to repeated declarations of neutrality from wings of the military, the liberal political class took them at their word and almost entirely *failed to reach out* beyond some scattered thanks. No plans were made to depose Trump and restore a centrist-liberal establishment government.

Instead, *once again*, the scattered remaining liberal leadership in Capitol Hill approached the President, in conjunction with some scared Republican congressmen, to collaborate on a "bipartisan plan for law and order." The thinking seems to have been that they could use this opportunity to wrangle acceptance of the courts back into the functional chain of command and wheedle Trump into suspending the Insurrection Act in some respects.

Their first and most impactful product was "feed nationalization." At the time a prominent plutocrat of the old empire, Elon Musk, had a third rate AI system he had financed as a vanity project to compete in the 20s' AI race, and it was rapidly rolled out to judge each post and decide whether they would ever be visible to anyone. He had captured a social media platform originally built by anarchists, “Twitter,” and this was abruptly rebranded as the "US Free Speech and Civil Discussion App" in rushed legislation and "The Everything App" on its actual interface. All other social media sites were blacklisted by BGP routers at major transaction points. And within 36 hours the blacklisting morphed into much more restrictive whitelisting. The liberals were successful in making sure this was all done over the course of two days via formal legislation and court order, with a non-partisan oversight board supposedly enshrined. It never convened.

The liberals truly believed they had turned the tide and wrestled back the beginning of control, but even more than the carnage in DC, this was the moment that functionally made the revolution. The changes to the social media ecosystem were so transparently heavyhanded that users immediately realized the remaining major platforms were totally useless, and, rather than cede to liberals' dream of a return to a pre-internet age, they mobilized.

While standard VPNs and at the time standard censorship circumvention tools had trouble accessing the external internet, enough ad hoc connectivity persisted over the first few days of implementation that alternative communication apps leaked through and were rapidly adopted. Many of these networks would spring into existence only to collapse within a day or two, unable to stand the infrastructural challenges at scale or taken out by firewall rules. Developers often worked in relative isolation, spitballing divergent responses to things they did not know were already solved problems. This rapid development of alternative communications network apps was sped up in part thanks to the simultaneous proliferation of local instances of early Chinese models.

For decades there was a widespread belief that the US revolution was provoked by the PRC in order to undermine frontier models in the AI development war that had been ratcheting across the 2020s, and the involvement of open source models like Deep Seek was often pointed to, but modern digital archeology paints a more complex picture. There was no PRC collusion of note with protesters in these early days, they just got lucky in the result of open-sourcing their models.

Indeed, the more dominant influences on the technical adoption patterns were paranoia storms, with several well-established, promising and/or quite valid technical options getting abruptly discarded.

But, for the purposes of understanding the revolution, there's one central technical feature: the alternative communication systems folks gravitated to required line of sight or close physical proximity to make connections between devices. And this forced more and more people who might have otherwise passively scrolled out of their houses.

As the infamous saying of the revolution went, "the internet is in the streets."

While the initial protests and occupations in November had been around the elections, the whitelist restrictions on the internet had the impact of turning almost everyone—including those who did not care about politics—out into street mobilizations.

In fact this massive mobilization was almost calamitous to the revolution. It was common for leftists and the first wave of liberal protesters to gripe during the second week of December that things had devolved into an almost completely apolitical street party.

Yet the success of these mass gatherings was dependent upon them being almost entirely made up of normal people, with explicitly political and high-information factions marginalized. Leftists complained bitterly about fascist co-option and populist narratives that threw out core progressive norms, values, and aspirations. But the numbers protected them from the kind of slaughters that had been escalating against the liberal and leftist protesters in November.

The demands at this point were basically "enough of this." And they might've been satisfied with the resignation of Trump and some military statements about returning normal civilian oversight and ending the economic crisis...

But the regime made no such concessions. Indeed it seemed incapable of making such concessions. And the more people spread into the streets, the more Trump and the actual decisionmaking circles in the palace of the old US empire—"the white house"—became desperate and paranoid. This led to proclamations and threats that sharpened the resolve and politicization of those in the streets.
